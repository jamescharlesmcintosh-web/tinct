VAT 04 · BERLIN   —

ABOUT THE VAT

We mix for the way
ink actually behaves.

Most ink is sold as a colour. Ours is sold as a behaviour — how it spreads on a wet press, how it holds an edge on glass, how it goes quiet next to real black. We make four at a time, in two litre batches, and we write down everything.

The vat is the whole company. There is no catalogue to leaf through and no swatch book to order. What you see moving is what is in the drum.

2 litres
REST
9 days before it ships
LEAD

RECORD

SIGNAL 01 · CYAN

Holds a 3px edge
without buzzing.

A cold cyan drawn for backlit glass. It is the only colour we make that looks better at night than in daylight, and we stopped apologising for that.

Mixed in two litre batches and rested nine days, which is longer than it needs and exactly as long as it wants.

BASE
Phthalocyanine, water-cut
SUBSTRATE
Etched glass · acrylic
EDGE
3 px minimum
BATCH
2 L

PRESS 04 · MAGENTA

It soaks.
It blooms. It stays honest.

Warm magenta cut for uncoated stock. On newsprint it does what newsprint does — spreads, softens, takes the grain with it — and the result is better than the proof every single time.

The oldest colour in the book. Batch 004 is the reason there is a company.

BASE
Quinacridone
SUBSTRATE
Uncoated · newsprint
SPREAD
12–18 μm
BATCH
2 L

LIGHT 02 · AMBER

Useless in daylight.
Unforgettable in the dark.

Fluorescent amber. It eats light and gives it back wrong, which is the whole point. We make one litre at a time because nobody has ever needed two.

Handle it under low light. The first time you see it on a wall at night you will understand the policy.

BASE
Fluorescent pigment, solvent
SUBSTRATE
Paper · matte wall
USE
Low light only
BATCH
1 L

NIGHT 07 · VIOLET

Reads as shadow

Near-black with a blue bias. Put it next to real black and it goes quiet and strange — the panel almost disappears, then the edge comes back.

Our most requested colour and our least favourite to explain.

SUBSTRATE
